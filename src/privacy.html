Privacy

Elsewhere has no accounts, no ads, no cookies, no trackingno tracking beyond anonymous counts of what happens in the game and no game server. This page lists everything that leaves your device, and who sees it.

In short

  • Your progress lives in your browser. Nobody asks for your email, your real name or your age.
  • When you play with other people, your game connects straight to theirs, so they can see your IP address. You can switch that off and play alone.
  • Leaderboard runs are public and permanent: they show the name you chose, your player ID and your score.
  • Chat and voice go straight to the other players in your world. The game never records or stores them.
  • Feedback you send from the Menu is encrypted so that only the team can read it.
  • Anonymous counts (how many people play, how far they get, how smoothly the game runs) carry no name, no player ID and no cookie. You can switch them off.

What stays on your device

Your browser keeps your progress for this site: the name and look you chose (and the looks you saved), your items and coins, how far you got in each world, your settings (including the players you blocked), runs waiting to be sent, Builder drafts, and the 3D models of community levels you have played. It also keeps your player key, a secret number that proves your runs are yours.

While anonymous counts are on (below), it also keeps the time of your last visit, so the count can say how long you were away; that time is never sent.

Clearing this site's data in your browser deletes all of it. The backup code (Menu, then You) holds the same things, key included, so keep it private. Pasting it into Restore on another device carries on where you left off.

Playing with other people

With Show me to other players on (it is on unless you switch it off, on the first screen or in the Menu under Online), your game connects directly to the games of the other players in the same world, peer to peer. They receive:

  • the name and look you chose (body style, face, outfit, colours), and what you are wearing;
  • your player ID (a short code made from your player key, shown next to your name so nobody can pass as you);
  • where you are and how you move;
  • what you write in chat, and your voice if you switch voice chat on.

Because the connection is direct, the other players' browsers learn your public IP address. An IP address can show roughly where you are (often your town or region) and who your internet provider is. Anyone who joins the same world with a copy of the game's code can see it too. If you would rather not share it, switch off Show me to other players: every world, the hunt and the leaderboards still work, just without other people.

To find each other, games leave short, scrambled connection offers on public message relays (listed below), and your browser asks an address server (STUN) for its public address. The relays see your IP address and a code for the world you are joining. They do not see your chat, your voice or your moves, which travel between the players' games.

Chat, voice, mute and block

  • Chat appears in the other players' games and is not saved anywhere. Your game hides swear words and slurs in other people's chat, names and level names, and takes links, email addresses and phone numbers out of chat. You can choose Quick chat only (ready-made phrases, nothing typed) or switch chat off (Menu, then Online).
  • Voice chat is off until you switch it on, and then it is push-to-talk unless you choose an open mic. Your voice goes only to the players nearest you in the same world. It is never recorded.
  • Mute a player to stop hearing them and seeing their chat. Block them and you disappear for each other. Press P (or tap the player count at the top left) to see who is here. Your block list stays on your device.

Leaderboards and saved progress

With Save my progress online on (Menu, then Online), each visit to a world is sent to public relays when you leave it. The record holds the name you chose, your player ID, the world, the keys you pressed and when, your time or score, what you found, and the date. A checker replays it and, if it adds up, adds it to the game's public leaderboard record, kept in the game's code repository on GitHub. That is how the leaderboards stay fair, and how your progress comes back on another device.

These records are public and permanent: anyone can read them, and the game cannot delete them from the relays or from copies other people keep. They do not contain your IP address, but the relays see it when your game sends a record. Switch Save my progress online off, and runs wait in your browser instead (they are not on the leaderboards).

Feedback you send

The Feedback tab in the Menu sends your message to the people making the game, only when you press Send. With it go: the world you are in, the minutes you have played this session, whether you are on a phone or a computer (screen size, browser, graphics setting), and where you are playing (the game's website, or itch.io). Not your name or player ID.

It travels through the same public relays as saved runs, but encrypted to the team's key: the relays, and anyone reading from them, see only scrambled text. The relays do see your IP address when your game sends it.

Anonymous counts

To learn how many people play, where they come from and where the game needs work, the game sends short anonymous counts to GoatCounter ({COUNTER_HOST}), an open-source counter that sets no cookies. Each count is one line from a fixed list, never anything you typed, and never your name or player ID:

  • a visit to the game, with the site that linked here (and a ref= tag in the link, if it had one), and whether you are playing on the website, on itch.io or inside another site's page;
  • a first visit's title screen and Play button, or for a returning player, roughly how long since the last visit (today, yesterday, within a week, a month, longer);
  • the worlds you arrive in, the items you find, the hunt's milestones, the story chapters you finish (and Chapter 1's choice), and the races, rides and scores you finish;
  • minutes played (one, five, fifteen, thirty, sixty), and once, after a minute, whether you are on a phone or a computer, the graphics setting, and the frame rate, rounded to a band;
  • that you sent feedback, with its mood (the words stay encrypted, above); that your saved progress needed catching up; and that the game failed to start or a world failed to load (no details).

With each count your browser sends what any web request carries: your IP address and browser details. From them GoatCounter works out the country, browser, system and language, and it stores only totals like these, never the IP address or an ID that follows you. To count one visitor once, it keeps the IP address and browser in memory (not on disk) for up to 8 hours, linked to a random number (how GoatCounter counts, its privacy policy). The team reads the totals on GoatCounter.

Nothing is counted when you switch Count my visits anonymously off (Menu, then Online), when your browser asks sites not to track you (Do Not Track or Global Privacy Control), or in automated browsers.

Levels you publish

Publishing a level from the Builder sends it, with the name you chose and your player ID, to the same public relays, where it stays. Playing someone's level can download its 3D models from those relays.

Who else is involved

  • GitHub Pages hosts the game, so GitHub sees your IP address and browser when the game loads, as any website host does (GitHub's privacy policies). The arcade cabinet's game, Siegeworks, is hosted the same way and loads only when you open the cabinet.
  • Message relays for meeting other players: nos.lol, relay.nostr.net, nostr.mom, relay.primal.net, relay.snort.social, nostr.rblb.it, relay.flashapp.me. For leaderboard runs, levels and models: nos.lol, relay.primal.net, relay.nostr.net, nostr.mom, relay.damus.io, relay.snort.social, relay.coinos.io. These are independent public Nostr relays, each run by its own operator under its own rules.
  • Address servers (STUN) run by Google (stun.l.google.com) and Cloudflare (stun.cloudflare.com), only while you are shown to other players.
  • GoatCounter ({COUNTER_HOST}) for the anonymous counts above, while they are on.
  • itch.io, if you play there. Their page shows the game in a frame, and the game inside it is this website's, sending only what this page describes. itch.io's page is covered by their privacy policy. Your progress there is kept apart from this website's, because browsers keep each site's saves separately; the backup code (Menu, then You) carries it across.

Fonts and every other file come from the game's own site. Nothing is sold or shared with advertisers, because nothing is collected to sell.

Children and parents

The game asks for no personal details. Pick a made-up name rather than your real one, and never share where you live or your phone number in chat. For a younger player, in the Menu under Online: set Text chat to Quick chat only or Off, leave voice chat off, or switch off Show me to other players to play with nobody else at all.

Questions and changes

The game's code is public, so anyone can check what it does: github.com/definitelynotai729-bit/elsewhere. Questions or problems: open an issue there. If what the game shares changes, this page changes with it.
