# THE LONG LIGHT

*A single-player space opera for Elsewhere, in ten chapters.*

**Logline.** You step through a portal into the Vellum, a galaxy whose rulers edit what everyone remembers, and you turn out to be the one person their record cannot see. A postmistress, two robots and a warrior who has stood frozen in light for sixty years need you to carry the truth the light still holds, and then to decide what a whole galaxy should remember.

## Tone and themes

**Tone.** Earnest, swashbuckling adventure with warmth and wit, mythic awe (you will walk on light) and real moral weight, never grim. Rated T: sparkers stun, blades knock people down, robots break, memories are taken. Nobody dies on screen; the deaths are history.

**Themes.**
1. **Memory is a right and a burden.** The empire's crime is taking it. The heroes' test is carrying it.
2. **Half a truth is a weapon.** The heroes' own tradition started the war by telling only half. Everyone in this galaxy holds a half.
3. **Agreement can't be imposed.** The Unanimity offers peace by consensus, and its consensus is edited into people while they sleep.
4. **Keeping a light for strangers.** The heroes are a lighthouse keeper, a postmistress and an archive clerk. Heroism here is a light left on for someone you'll never meet.
5. **Unseen is free; seen is belonging.** You start as nobody, invisible to the system. You end as someone the galaxy remembers, or chooses not to.

It suits Elsewhere, where records are proof and nobody can quietly rewrite what happened. The story never preaches it; it just happens to be about it.

## The player character

- **Who.** A traveller from Elsewhere with no history in the Vellum. You speak through choices, which give you an attitude: warm, wry or blunt.
- **What you are, in-world.** A *null entry*: someone without a **tally**, the glass bead everyone in the Vellum carries behind one ear. Censor drones read tallies, so they can't see you. And because tallies dull the Stilling, stilled light forms in your hands.
- **Not a chosen one.** Sixty years ago a Keeper lit a lamp "for anyone unwritten", and you are simply the first through. "I didn't call *you*. I called *anyone*. You came."
- **Your name.** In Chapter 1 an archive robot must file you: **Stranger**, **Nobody** or **Elsewhere**. The crew uses it (`{call}`).
- **Why you stay.** The Unanimity will hunt a person it can't see, and the crew are in trouble because of you. Then the light starts showing you things.
- **How you get here from Junction.**
  - A hand-placed arch of old lamp-glass with a rose-gold lens stands near the Creator's Gate, labelled **THE LONG LIGHT**, *a story in ten chapters*. Wren: "That one hums like a lighthouse. Nobody knows who put it there."
  - At first it leads into a crate in the mail ship *Rumour*'s hold; after Chapter 1 it leads to the *Rumour* itself, the campaign's hub.
  - Aboard, the **Nowhere Lens** (a shard of that lamp) leads back to Junction at any time.

## Setting

### The Vellum
A spiral galaxy of a thousand lit worlds. Its rich core is **the Binding**, its poor fringe is **the Margin**, and the space between the lanes is **the Unlit**.

### Light remembers
- Every ray of light carries the image of what it touched, and almost all of that scatters.
- A mind in perfect stillness can ask some light to *stop*. **Stilled light** keeps its picture forever. It can be read, and shaped into a blade, a step or a bridge.
- In a lens or shard it is **stillglass**. It cannot lie, but it can be *cut*: someone chooses which part anyone sees.
- The discipline is **the Stilling**.

### Lamps and lanes
- Ships run **lanes**, roads of held light kept open by **lamps**: stillglass lighthouses at the edges of star systems.
- A **lane-drive** rides a lamp's beam. Jumping into a lane is **lighting out**.
- An **unlit lane** is possible, and dangerous.
- Whoever holds the lamps holds the roads.

### Tallies, the Fair Copy and the night edit
- Everyone born in the Binding is fitted with a **tally** in infancy. It reports to **the Fair Copy**, the single official record kept in **the Scriptorium** on the capital world **Plenary**.
- At night, near a lamp-relay, the tally rewrites memories that disagree with the Fair Copy: **the night edit**. People wake up agreeing.
- **Sleep in the Unlit and you wake up yourself.** That is why dissidents live in the Margin.
- Reading a note in your own handwriting can break an edit. Such notes are **margin slips**.

### History
| when | what happened |
|---|---|
| ~400 years ago | Lamp-tenders discover the Stilling in their lenses. They become **the Lightkeepers**, the Vellum's witnesses, and build **the Ammonite**, a spiral archive station holding **the Long Light**: every record they ever stilled. |
| 92 years ago | **Saltglass.** On the salt moon Saltglass, Coalition soldiers fire on a crowd at the Brinewell, and three thousand die. Keeper **Orla Quaile** stills the shooting and releases it through the lamps. The same night she finds a *painted* stillglass, forged by the separatist Brine Congress, that had told the crowd the soldiers poisoned the well. She buries the forgery, because she believes it would only excuse the massacre. |
| 92–62 years ago | **The Clamour.** The released half lights a war. Every world answers with its own half of something. Thirty years of records used as weapons. |
| 62 years ago | **The Assent.** Quaile's former student, **Benedikta Sallow**, has found the buried half. She concludes that people can't be trusted with records at all, and proposes one shared record (the Fair Copy), kept gently, with tallies to help everyone agree. Exhausted worlds sign. The **Unanimity** is born and Sallow becomes **the Moderator**. |
| 60 years ago | **The Dimming.** The Lightkeepers are outlawed ("record-makers are war-makers"). The Ammonite is quenched and hidden. Keepers are tallied or flee. Quaile lights a lamp on Vitrine that opens onto Elsewhere, "for anyone unwritten", then stills herself in the ice of Skerrow with the whole truth of Saltglass in her eyes. |
| 20 years ago | **Sallow dies.** Her deputy, the young Redactor **Ansel Leth**, hides her death. A stilled-light recording of her keeps giving the weekly Moderator's Hour, and Leth rises to Redactor-General. |
| now (year 62 of the Assent) | The night edits fail more often every year, because there is too much paper in the Margin. Leth plans **the Last Edit**: on **Assent Day**, make every tally's edit permanent and erase the Margin from memory. To do it he needs the Ammonite's **Heartglass** and an untallied hand to set it. |

## Factions

### The Unanimity
The authoritarian power: polite, prosperous at its centre, smothering at its edges.
- **Look.** White stone, gold trim, and rows of identical windows like lines of text.
- **Slogan.** *We agree.* Its creed, the Assent: *"We agree; we remember; we are one record."*
- **Ayes.** Conscript soldiers in a light trance called *assent*. Ivory plate, an oval faceplate crossed by one gold line, and grey tabards printed with the Assent, like walking pages. They speak in the plural and fire **sparkers** (stun bolts).
- **Censors.** Flying drones: white spheres with a black redaction bar across the face. They read tallies and cannot see you.
- **Redactors.** Officers in grey coats, ink-black gloves and blank paper masks, carrying **quench-staves** that pour out a bar of light-eating dark.
- **Ships.** **Gavels** (blunt landing craft), **quills** (needle fighters) and the flagship ***Final Word***, a city-sized closed book with a gold-lit spine.

### The Margin
The scrappy resistance, named for the fringe it lives on.
- **Cells** are scattered across the poor worlds. Their weapon is paper.
- **Members** write slips to their future, edited selves and smuggle them home.
- **Symbol.** A single vertical stroke, like a margin rule.
- **Saying.** *Write it in the margin.*
- **Leadership.** On Plenary, a reclusive printer called **the Printer**.
- **Circulation.** Its bloodstream is **the Unlit Post**, the last independent mail route in the Vellum, flown by one stubborn captain.

### The Lightkeepers
The ancient warrior tradition, almost extinct.
- **Origins.** They began as lamp-tenders, and "Keeper" still means both a lighthouse keeper and a sworn witness.
- **Ranks.** Spark (novice), Lamp (sworn), Keeper, First Keeper.
- **Oath.** *What the light saw, I will say.*
- **The three tenets.** *Still the hand, that it strikes true. Still the eye, that it sees whole. Still the heart, that it holds light.* Quaile broke the second one at Saltglass.
- **The forms.**
  1. The Stilled Edge: the stillblade.
  2. The Second Step: stilling light under your foot in mid-air.
  3. The Veil: stilling the light around you so it holds you as you fall.
  4. The Witness: reading stillglass and waking the memory it holds.
  5. The Holding: stilling a moving beam into a road.
- **Parting words.** "Light enough to walk by."

### The Uncounted
The raft-clans of the ocean world **Pelagia**. They never took tallies, and the Unanimity leaves them alone because they are "too poor to edit".
- **Names.** They take bird names.
- **Habits.** They go barefoot, sing tide-songs and settle disputes over kelp tea.
- **Sayings.** *Let the tide decide. The sea doesn't count.*

### Clerks
Every robot in the Vellum is a **clerk**, because the first ones kept records. "A clerk who stopped filing" means a rebel.

### The Myrr
Beings made of thousands of luminous moths flying in a person's shape. Each moth carries part of the person's memory. Scattered, a Myrr forgets who it is.

## Cast

1. **The player: {call}, the Unentered.** See above.
   - *Voice:* the player's choice of warm, wry or blunt.
   - *Arc:* from invisible stranger to the one who decides what the Vellum remembers.

2. **Errata (Archive Clerk ER-8).** A thin archive robot with a brass lens-eye, sixty-one years at the Lamp of Hesk inspection office. It "defects" in Chapter 1, but secretly it is under orders from the Redactor-General: *accompany the null entry, report nightly, do not alert.*
   - *Voice:* deadpan and precise. "Noted." "Correction:" "I have a protocol for this situation. I have decided not to use it." It calls itself *it*: "It is a perfectly good word."
   - *Arc:* from obedient redactor to keeper of its own memory. Exposed in Chapter 5, it is either forgiven (it breaks its own seal, painfully) or reset to a cheerful blank.

3. **Pockets.** A knee-high mail-sorting robot with six arms and far too many pockets. It speaks only in words it has found on labels and signs: "HELLO. FRAGILE. THIS WAY UP."
   - *Voice:* all capitals, telegraphic, oddly profound.
   - *Arc:* it collects words all campaign. In the last scene it says the only sentence of its own it ever will, built from the words you found.
   - It has no tally (clerks never do), which is why it remembers you in every ending.

4. **Captain Ottoline "Otto" Brack.** Eighty-one, captain of the *Rumour*. She runs the **Unlit Post**, letters on paper between people the Fair Copy has separated. At nineteen she was the courier who carried the Assent treaty to Plenary.
   - *Voice:* gruff and warm, with northern idioms ("pet", "love", "right, then"). She hums old lane-songs and swears by the post: "Post's post. I don't read it."
   - *Arc:* from "I carry letters, not causes" to choosing a cause. In Chapter 2 she finds a sixty-two-year-old letter in her own handwriting that she doesn't remember writing.

5. **Keeper Orla Quaile.** A Lightkeeper who stilled herself in ice sixty years ago. She is fifty-nine in body and a hundred and nineteen by the calendar.
   - *Voice:* sharp, formal and sixty years out of date ("A moderator of *what*?"). She calls everyone "child", swallows her grief whole and has a dry, unexpected humour.
   - *Arc:* the mentor who is wrong about something important. She released half the truth of Saltglass and started a war. She does not die. She chooses to surrender herself to buy the Margin's fleet a lane, and at the end she has to decide, with you, whether she still trusts the whole truth.

6. **Redactor-General Ansel Leth.** Forty-six, the de facto ruler of the Unanimity, born **Petrel** on the Pelagian raft-city Saltwort. At twelve he watched a true record, posted by the Margin, tear his clan apart; his parents' raft was cut loose in a storm. He swam to a Unanimity patrol boat and asked for a tally so he could forget. Later he removed himself from the Fair Copy, so that nobody could ever edit him again. Like you, he is unentered.
   - *Voice:* soft, courteous, apologetic, never raised. He uses Pelagian tide idioms and walks barefoot on his own flagship. "I'm sorry. This won't hurt. You won't remember that it did."
   - *Arc:* he believes truth kills and kindness is a quiet room. He has hidden his mentor's death, and her last wish, for twenty years. He can be defeated, spared, or (if you learned his true name) offered the light.

7. **The Moderator, Benedikta Sallow.** The Unanimity's founder and Quaile's student. She has been dead for twenty years. What the Vellum sees every week is her **stilled-light recording**, cycling through forty phrases.
   - *Voice:* warm, maternal, measured. "Agreement is a kindness we do for each other."
   - *Arc (posthumous):* her real last message, hidden by Leth, is the key to the climax: "Tell them everything. I built a quiet room and called it peace."

8. **Dace Mirrin.** Nineteen, a Margin printer from Amaranth, and the Printer's grandson.
   - *Voice:* fast, angry and poetic, full of printing words ("set it in type", "run it", "bold face").
   - *Arc:* he learns that truth is not a club to swing. Whether he prints early (Chapter 4) changes the war.

9. **Thousand.** A Myrr archivist who hides in the ruined Lightkeeper school on Amaranth.
   - *Voice:* gentle, always "we", sentences that trail off when moths are missing: "We remember the... we did remember."
   - *Arc:* scattered by Censors in Chapter 4. Every moth you find gives back a memory. In Chapter 6 it may choose to stay in the Ammonite as its keeper.

10. **Mother Skua.** Matriarch of Saltwort, Leth's aunt.
    - *Voice:* blunt, funny and loud. She sings while she bargains and calls people "barnacle".
    - *Arc:* she hates outsiders, then trusts you, then has to face the nephew she thought had drowned.

11. **Tern.** Fifteen, a Pelagian diver and Mother Skua's grandchild.
    - *Voice:* cocky, quick and competitive: "Race you."
    - *Arc:* races you up the Old Lamp and memorises the way to the Ammonite. Depending on your choice in Chapter 5, Tern guides you, or is taken and tallied and can be woken with a slip in Chapter 9.

12. **Tobin ("Aye Forty-One").** A conscript Aye on Skerrow.
    - *Voice:* "we" when in assent, a halting "I" when woken. He keeps remembering a dog.
    - *Arc:* if you help him in Chapter 3, he remembers his name, and later he opens a gate at Cinder Gate and leads the Ayes who stand down on Plenary.

13. **Ampersand.** An ancient filing clerk on Plenary whose only job for twenty years has been filing the Moderator's weekly remarks.
    - *Voice:* one endless sentence joined by "and": "and she said number twelve again and I filed it and I have filed number twelve two hundred and six times and"
    - *Arc:* lonely and loyal, it gives you proof that the Moderator is dead, and then gets to stop.

14. **Juniper Mirrin, the Printer.** Eighty-six, leader of the Margin, living in Plenary's lowest levels, known as **the Footnotes**.
    - *Voice:* wry, slow and ink-stained. "Paper remembers what people are told to forget."
    - *Arc:* she has run a rebellion by post for forty years, and now has one night to print the most important slip ever written.

15. **Redactor Imre Vane.** A mid-ranking Redactor who keeps turning up a step behind you.
    - *Voice:* pompous and officious; he cites ordinances that may not exist ("In accordance with the Restricted Flats Ordinance...").
    - *Arc:* comic menace in Act I. In Act III you meet him again as an Aye who doesn't remember being a Redactor. Leth edited him for failing: "It's kinder."

## The plot

### Act I: The Unentered

1. **Stowaway** (the *Rumour*, docked at the Lamp of Hesk).
   - You step out of a crate during a Unanimity inspection. Censors can't see you; the Ayes can. You hide Brack's paper post under their noses.
   - The clerk Errata finds you by weighing the ship and files you under the name you choose.
   - Leth's voice comes over the lamp. You free the docking clamps, and the *Rumour* lights out into the Unlit.
   - The shard you came through still holds a picture: a woman in ice, a lamp in a glass desert.

2. **The Lamp That Points Nowhere** (Vitrine, desert).
   - In the glass-mining town of Glassgut, Brack finds a dead letter in her own handwriting: at the Assent the Moderator wept and said "Forgive me, Orla."
   - You may hide the miners' slips from a Censor sweep.
   - Climbing over a gate that cannot count you, you cross the Flats to the ruined far-lamp. Quaile's sixty-year-old message gives you a dark hilt and a destination.
   - Ayes close in, and you ride a salvage sled down the Long Dune to the *Rumour*.

3. **The Woman in the Ice** (Skerrow, ice).
   - You climb a crevassed glacier to an occupied observatory and perhaps wake a conscript, Tobin.
   - You align the observatory's great mirrors to pour starlight down a well into the ice, and Quaile wakes, swinging.
   - She teaches you to still a blade from a light of your choosing, then the Second Step, as the glacier calves under Vane's guns.

### Act II: The Half-Truth

4. **What the Flowers Saw** (Amaranth, jungle).
   - At the ruined Lightkeeper school, afterbloom flowers replay old light after dark. You run the Lamp Trial behind the ghost of a fourteen-year-old Benedikta Sallow.
   - You gather the scattered moths of Thousand.
   - In the Keeper's garden the flowers show Quaile cutting a stillglass in half, and she confesses Saltglass.
   - Dace wants to print what the flowers saw.
   - You learn the Veil from the bell tower and glide out through a burning canopy.

5. **The Uncounted** (Pelagia, ocean).
   - You race Tern up a sunken lighthouse before the tide returns, and find the way to the Ammonite carved in its lens.
   - Leth arrives in person and drinks kelp tea at Mother Skua's table, and she calls him by the name he was born with: Petrel.
   - That night his Ayes raid Saltwort in a storm. You must choose between the children's raft and the stolen chart.
   - Pockets' evidence exposes Errata as Leth's informant, and you decide what happens to it.

6. **The Long Light** (the Ammonite, derelict).
   - In a spiral station adrift in the Hush nebula, your blade's light wakes **memory-bridges**: floors that collapsed decades ago, remembered in light and solid again.
   - You walk inward, back through time, to the Saltglass chamber and see both halves.
   - You lift the Heartglass, and every lamp in the Vellum flickers. Leth announces the Last Edit for Assent Day.

### Act III: The Fair Copy

7. **Lampfall** (Cinder Gate, a lamp on the edge of a red giant).
   - The lamps are going dark. You climb the last great lamp that still holds a lane to Plenary, dodging stellar flares, and hold its beam open for the Margin fleet.
   - Leth comes to the gallery alone. To buy the fleet its passage, Quaile gives him herself and the Heartglass.

8. **The City That Agrees** (Plenary, city-world).
   - From the Footnotes you ride the transit rails of a city that stops and speaks in unison every hour.
   - You find Ampersand and its proof that the Moderator is dead, and set the type for the Margin's one great slip.
   - You loose a blizzard of paper over the skyline the night before Assent Day.

9. **The Fair Copy** (the Scriptorium).
   - You climb the archive spire, helped by every ally your choices earned, to the Moderator's Hall. Her image casts no shadow, and you unstill her true last message.
   - You duel Leth on the crown of the spire above the clouds.
   - At the master lamp, Quaile is holding the Heartglass and waiting for you. You decide what the Vellum will remember.

10. **Margins** (Vitrine, after). Months later the *Rumour* brings you back to the far-lamp you first came through. Everyone you met is where your choices left them. Pockets says its sentence, and the lamp offers you the way home.

## Endings

Every ending is chosen at the master lamp in Chapter 9 and shown in Chapter 10. All four are always on offer; earlier flags change how each one lands.

1. **Full Light.** The Heartglass broadcasts the whole Long Light to every tally, and every edit is undone at once. Everyone sees both halves of Saltglass, the Moderator's death and her last message, and every half-truth the Unanimity, the Margin and the Lightkeepers ever told.
   - *The Loud Years.* The Unanimity dissolves inside a week, and some worlds come to the edge of war.
   - Earlier choices decide who steadies it:
     - Pelagian mediators, if you saved the raft;
     - unmasked Ayes, if Tobin woke;
     - Leth testifying, if spared.
   - *Title: Full Light.*
2. **The Open Library.** Every tally is unsealed, so the night edit ends, but nothing is broadcast. The Long Light becomes a library anyone may visit, and change comes one reader at a time.
   - Thousand is librarian at the Ammonite if it stayed. Otherwise the Scriptorium becomes the reading room.
   - *Title: Librarian of the Long Light.*
3. **The Kept Light.** You and Quaile keep the Heartglass and re-found the Lightkeepers. The Last Edit never fires, and truths are released as you judge wise.
   - The Unanimity survives, weakened, with a conscience it can't edit. It is gentler, and run by people deciding what others can bear, which is how the Unanimity began.
   - Pockets' sentence ends with a question mark.
   - *Title: Keeper.*
4. **The Fair Copy.** You agree with Leth and set a kinder copy permanently. The Clamour is forgotten, the Margin forgiven rather than erased, and the peace is real.
   - Your tallied friends forget you: Brack, Mags, Tobin. Clerks have no tallies, so Pockets and Errata remember, and Quaile's stilled mind keeps.
   - *Title: Fair Copyist.*

- *Lamps*, never "beacons".
- *The Veil*, not "the Drift" (a film's mind-link).
- *Plenary*, not "Quorum" (a fictional council).
- A crate of lens-glass, not a ring dug out of a desert.

**Not the Star Wars plot shape.**
- The hero is a stranger from outside the galaxy, not a farm youth.
- The mentor is flawed, started the war, and lives.
- There is no princess. The one person you reach at the end is a hundred-and-nineteen-year-old who isn't waiting to be rescued.
- There is no superweapon. The climax is a choice about memory.
- The ruler is a dead woman's recording.

**Distinct designs.**
- Ships: a post-red mail hauler, and a flagship shaped like a closed book.
- Soldiers in printed tabards; drones with a censor's bar.
- Warriors who stand on light.
